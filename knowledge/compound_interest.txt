WHAT IS COMPOUND INTEREST

NOTE ON INVESTOR.GOV CLAIMS: The two sections below ("WHAT IS COMPOUND
INTEREST" and "HOW COMPOUND INTEREST WORKS IN SAVINGS") are attributed to
the Investor.gov article "What is compound interest?" (Source A above).
The page title was confirmed but the body text was not retrieved. The
definitions given are standard and widely used, but they have not been
verified against the full text of that specific page. They are presented
here as attributed but unverified.

[INVESTOR.GOV -- not body-verified] Compound interest is interest calculated
on both the initial principal and the interest that has already been earned
on that principal. In other words, it is "interest on interest."

[INVESTOR.GOV -- not body-verified] This is distinct from simple interest,
which is calculated only on the original principal amount.

The following claims ARE confirmed from the CFPB credit card key terms page:

[CFPB-CC] A credit card's interest rate is typically stated as an annual
percentage rate (APR). The APR is the standard way to compare how much
loans or credit products cost on an apples-to-apples basis.

[CFPB-CC] For credit cards, interest is calculated using a daily periodic
rate. This interest amount is added to the previous day's balance, which
means that interest on credit card balances is compounded on a daily basis.

[CFPB-CC] APRs are often different from simple interest rates. The APR
accounts for the interest rate and fees compared against the amount
borrowed over a one-year period.


HOW COMPOUND INTEREST WORKS IN SAVINGS

[INVESTOR.GOV -- not body-verified] When interest compounds on a savings
account or investment, the earned interest is added to the balance, and
future interest is calculated on that larger balance. Each compounding
period, the balance grows by a larger absolute dollar amount than the
period before.

[INVESTOR.GOV -- not body-verified] Compounding frequency matters: the more
frequently interest is applied (for example, monthly versus annually), the
faster a balance grows, even at the same stated annual rate.


APR AND APY: WHAT IS THE DIFFERENCE

APR and APY are not interchangeable. They measure different things:

[CFPB-CC] The APR (annual percentage rate) is the stated annual cost of
BORROWING. It is used to compare loan and credit products. It includes
the interest rate and fees calculated over one year.

APY (annual percentage yield) is the effective annual rate of RETURN on a
savings account or investment after accounting for how often interest
compounds within the year. When interest compounds more than once per year
(for example, monthly), the APY is higher than the nominal annual rate.

APR is used in the context of debt (what you pay). APY is used in the
context of savings and deposits (what you earn). They are computed
differently and cannot be compared directly.

NOTE: APY is a standard financial definition included here for completeness.
The CFPB credit card key terms page (Source B) defines APR clearly and is
the source of the APR claim above. APY is not discussed in that retrieved
text. The APY explanation is not drawn from a confirmed retrieved source.


HOW FINPATH AI USES COMPOUND INTEREST

[FINPATH] The project_savings_growth tool calculates future value using
monthly compounding. The formula applies one-twelfth of the annual interest
rate each month:

  Monthly rate (r) = annual_rate / 12

  When r > 0 (the normal case):
    Balance after N months = principal * (1 + r)^N
                           + monthly_contribution * ((1 + r)^N - 1) / r

  When annual_rate == 0 (zero-interest case):
    Balance after N months = principal + monthly_contribution * N

The zero-interest branch is handled explicitly in the code (tools/core/finance.py,
lines 143-144) to avoid a division-by-zero error in the annuity formula.

[FINPATH] CONTRIBUTION TIMING: The formula above is the future value of an
ordinary annuity, meaning each monthly_contribution is assumed to occur at
the END of the month. The principal earns compound growth for the full N
months, while each contribution earns growth only for the months remaining
after it is made. An annuity-due (beginning-of-month) formula would include
an additional (1 + r) multiplier; the tool does not apply that multiplier.

[FINPATH] The tool requires annual interest rates to be supplied as decimals.
For example, 5% per year is entered as 0.05, not 5. The tool raises an error
if a rate greater than 1 is supplied.

[FINPATH] The default annual growth rate when a user does not specify one is
5% per year (0.05). This default is always stated in the agent response.


KEY TAKEAWAYS

- Compound interest means earning interest on previously accumulated
  interest, not just the original principal. [Investor.gov -- not body-verified]
- For credit card debt, interest compounds daily, increasing the amount
  owed faster than simple interest would. [CFPB-CC -- confirmed]
- APR (annual percentage rate) measures the cost of borrowing per year.
  APY (annual percentage yield) measures the effective annual return on
  savings after compounding. They are not interchangeable.
- For savings and investments, more frequent compounding produces a higher
  effective annual return than the stated nominal rate alone.
- FinPath AI's project_savings_growth tool uses monthly compounding and
  handles the zero-interest edge case with simple accumulation.
